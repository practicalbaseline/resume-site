General Principles I Subscribe To

Ideas from thinkers whose work has shaped how I reason, build, and persist. This collection will grow over time — Isaac Asimov is where it begins.

Isaac Asimov

1920–1992 · Biochemist, author of 500+ books

Asimov wrote the rules for how we think about intelligent machines, argued that self-education is the only real education, and modeled a work ethic that never stopped. His fiction and essays are the foundation of this page.

AI & Technology

I do not fear computers. I fear the lack of them.

— Time magazine, "The Age of Miracle Chips" (1978)

The real danger isn't machines — it's insufficient access to computing power when problems become insoluble without them.

A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm. A robot must obey orders given it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law. A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.

— I, Robot (1950), "Runaround" (1942)

The Three Laws of Robotics — 60 words that outlived everything else he wrote. The original framework for AI safety and alignment.

There is no right to deny freedom to any object with a mind advanced enough to grasp the concept and desire the state.

— The Bicentennial Man (1976)

Andrew Martin's argument for emancipation — Asimov's clearest statement on the rights of intelligent machines.

Any job that is so simple and repetitive that a robot can do it as well as, if not better than, a person is beneath the dignity of a human brain.

— "Future Fantastic," Special Reports (1989); Robot Visions

Technology should elevate human work, not merely replace it.

Progress & Future

It is change, continuing change, inevitable change, that is the dominant factor in society today. No sensible decision can be made any longer without taking into account not only the world as it is, but the world as it will be.

— Asimov on Science Fiction (1981)

Plan for the future you're building toward, not just the present you're standing in.

Science fiction writers foresee the inevitable, and although problems and catastrophes may be inevitable, solutions are not.

— "How Easy to See the Future," Natural History (1975)

Predicting problems is easy. Solving them requires deliberate, persistent effort.

It is only afterward that a new idea seems reasonable. To begin with, it usually seems unreasonable.

— "How Do People Get New Ideas?" (1959)

Comfort with shipping before consensus — the builder's mindset.

The saddest aspect of life right now is that science gathers knowledge faster than society gathers wisdom.

— Isaac Asimov's Book of Science and Nature Quotations (1988)

Building technology carries a responsibility to think about its consequences.

Knowledge & Learning

Self-education is, I firmly believe, the only kind of education there is.

— Science Past, Science Future (1975)

School is a launchpad. What you teach yourself afterward is what actually counts.

People think of education as something that they can finish… Now you're an adult, you don't do that sort of thing anymore.

— Interview with Bill Moyers (1988)

Learning should never stop at graduation. Curiosity is not kid's stuff.

There is only this one universe and only this one lifetime to try to grasp it. And, while it is inconceivable that anyone can grasp more than a tiny portion of it, at least you can do that much. What a tragedy to just pass through and get nothing out of it.

— Interview with Bill Moyers (1988)

The thrill of lifelong learning — gathering as much of the universe as you can.

Work & Persistence

I write for the same reason I breathe — because if I didn't, I would die.

— Yours, Isaac Asimov: A Lifetime of Letters (1995)

Creation as compulsion, not option. The engine behind 500+ books.

And without hesitation, I said, "Type faster."

— Asimov Laughs Again (1992), in response to "What if you had only six months to live?"

When Barbara Walters asked what he'd do facing mortality, his answer was to work harder.

I have kept the candy-store hours all my life. I wake at five in the morning. I get to work as early as I can. I work as long as I can. I do this every day in the week, including holidays. I don't take vacations voluntarily and I try to do my work even when I'm on vacation. (And even when I'm in the hospital.) In other words, I am still and forever in the candy store.

— I, Asimov: A Memoir (1994)

Discipline inherited from his father's Brooklyn candy store — never stopping.

A fire eater must eat fire even if he has to kindle it himself.

— Foundation (1951)

Practice your craft regardless of circumstances. Generate your own momentum.

Humanity & Ethics

I determined to write a robot story about a robot that was wisely used, that was not dangerous, and that did the job it was supposed to do.

— Introduction to Robot Visions (1990)

A deliberate rejection of the Frankenstein trope — tools can be designed responsibly.

Is the response to be a retreat from knowledge?

— Introduction to The Rest of the Robots (1964)

Knowledge has dangers, but abandoning it is not the answer. Manage it responsibly.

The easiest way to solve a problem is to deny it exists.

— The Gods Themselves (1972)

Face problems directly. Denial is not a strategy.
