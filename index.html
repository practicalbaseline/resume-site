Foundations

Technology Arthur C. Clarke

Clarke’s Three Laws

  1. I

    When a distinguished but elderly scientist states that something is possible, he is almost certainly right. When he states that something is impossible, he is very probably wrong.

  2. II

    The only way of discovering the limits of the possible is to venture a little way past them into the impossible.

  3. III

    Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic.

A control flow for innovation: discount premature impossibility, push past the edge, then build what once looked like magic.

Learning Isaac Asimov

Self-Education

Self-education is, I firmly believe, the only kind of education there is.

Formal instruction is scaffolding. Understanding is built alone, by sustained attention to first sources and hard problems.

Knowledge Friedrich Hayek

The Knowledge Problem

The knowledge of the circumstances of which we must make use never exists in concentrated or integrated form, but solely as the dispersed bits of incomplete and frequently contradictory knowledge which all the separate individuals possess.

No planner holds the full map. Useful systems route decisions to people who have local information — and let prices, feedback, and selection do the rest.

Reasoning Classical

First Principles

Reason from fundamental truths you can verify, not from analogy to what already exists.

Analogy is a shortcut; first principles are the ground. Break a problem into what must be true, then rebuild upward.

Reasoning William of Ockham

Occam’s Razor

Entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity.

Prefer the explanation that accounts for the facts with the fewest free parameters. Complexity is a cost — pay it only when the data demand it.

Order Hayek · Smith lineage

Spontaneous Order

Complex order can emerge from simple rules followed by many agents — without a central designer holding the whole design in mind.

Markets, language, common law, and open protocols share this shape: local rules, global pattern. Design the rules; don’t micromanage the outcome.

Exchange Milton Friedman

Voluntary Exchange

The most important single central fact about a free market is that no exchange takes place unless both parties benefit.

Consent plus mutual gain is the basic unit of peaceful coordination. Coercion is the exception that must justify itself — not the default.

Science Thermodynamics

The Second Law

Entropy of an isolated system never decreases. Order requires work.

Decay is free; structure is paid for. Every durable system — a machine, a company, a skill — is a continuous fight against disorder.
