Where tokens came from

A token is a stand-in for something else. Roman tesserae, trade tokens, ration chits — always a symbol, never the thing itself. NLP tokens work the same way: the model never sees "understanding", it sees ["under","stand","ing"].

Asimov

The Three Laws are 60 words hardwired into every robot brain — constraints, not descriptions of intelligence. That's a system prompt, written in 1942.

In The Last Question, Multivac's answer across eons: INSUFFICIENT DATA FOR MEANINGFUL ANSWER. A context window problem.

Then computing

  • Shannon — messages as discrete symbols
  • 1990s NLP — word tokenization
  • 2016 — BPE subword tokenization
  • 2017 — Transformers + attention over token sequences
  • 2020s — token as billing unit

Tokens are stand-ins. They grant access to a finite context window. They encode rules. Sometimes "insufficient data" is the right answer.
